Embr Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

Embr is an independent client for watching Twitch streams and chat on iPhone, iPad and Apple TV. It has no advertising and no tracking, and it keeps no analytics about what you watch or say. Your settings, watch history and block lists stay on your device. What reaches Midgar's servers is an anonymous Midgar ID, the purchase record if you leave a tip or buy Founding Supporter or Studio Pro, your Twitch user id when you claim Founding Supporter, the Twitch sign-in code or refresh token that Embr's server exchanges and does not keep, what you send when you report or block, the requests that play what you watch, fetch a past broadcast's chat and read a channel's poll or prediction, and a few anonymous counts. What reaches Twitch and a few emote and chat-history services is what a Twitch client has to send them to work.

In short

  • Your settings, favorites, watch history and muted words are stored on your device, and we never receive them. Your block list is stored on your device too, but a block sends the blocked person's name and ID, and the message if there is one, to us, as described under Reports and blocks.
  • The app keeps an anonymous Midgar ID, a random identifier with no name, email address or other detail about you, in your iCloud Keychain. Linking Sign in with Apple in another Midgar app adds that Apple account's identifier to the ID on our server. If that account is already linked to another Midgar ID, this device's ID is merged into that other one, and the device then uses it, with its tips and purchases.
  • When you leave a tip or buy Founding Supporter or Studio Pro, the purchase record from Apple is sent to Midgar's own server, linked to that Midgar ID.
  • Embr Studio reads your own channel's events straight from Twitch; its recaps and settings stay on your device.
  • The app sends anonymous counts with no identifier: opening the More Apps screen in Settings (opened, shown, tapped, loaded, launched), opening the Studio Pro purchase screen, and how many people use the app each day, week and month.
  • Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

What stays on your device

Your settings, favorite channels, watch history, recent channels, muted keywords and blocked users are stored in the app's private storage on your device. Your favorite channels are also kept in your own iCloud account through Apple's iCloud key-value storage, so the channels you star appear on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account; Apple stores them for you and Midgar never receives them. On an Apple TV with several people, each person's favorites and Twitch sign-in stay in their own profile. A diagnostic log is written there too. It records errors (some with the status of a failed request or the address of an image that failed to load), the Twitch names of the accounts you sign in with, the channels you open and the people named in reports you send, but never your Twitch token. It leaves your device only if you choose Share Diagnostic Logs in Settings. If you sign in with Twitch, your Twitch access and refresh tokens are stored in the device Keychain. Logging out removes them. Deleting your account in Settings also erases the rest of Embr's local data.

Sign in with Twitch

Signing in is optional and uses Twitch's official OAuth flow. Embr never sees your Twitch password. On Apple TV you sign in with Twitch's device code: the TV shows a code, you approve it on twitch.tv/activate on your phone, and Twitch gives the TV its tokens directly. When you sign in, the authorization code passes through Embr's Cloudflare Worker at embr.guitaripod.workers.dev on its way back to the app, and the app then posts it to Embr's server at api.midgarcorp.cc, a Cloudflare Worker that exchanges it with Twitch and returns the result to your device. When your session is renewed, the refresh token passes through api.midgarcorp.cc the same way. Neither keeps a copy of the tokens. Requests for your follows, the list of live channels and live chat then go from your device straight to Twitch with your access token. Deleting your account in Settings revokes Embr's access at Twitch.

Video, chat and emotes

Streams, video-on-demand, clips and chat come from Twitch. Your device sends live chat, video and clip files straight to Twitch, and when you are signed in, its own Twitch requests carry your access token, so Twitch receives your IP address for them. Embr's server makes the other requests for you: the playback addresses, the playlists described below, the active poll and prediction of each channel you open, and the chat replay of a video. Twitch sees only that server's address for those. Twitch's own privacy policy governs what it collects, including through its embedded player. To show emotes, the app asks 7TV, BetterTTV and FrankerFaceZ for the emote sets of the channel you are watching, and it asks the public recent-messages service at recent-messages.robotty.de for a channel's recent chat. Those services receive the channel name or id and your IP address. Embr sends them nothing that identifies you.

To play a stream, video or clip, the app asks Embr's own server at api.midgarcorp.cc for it. The server asks Twitch for the playback address, then fetches the playlists from Twitch while you watch, removes the advertisements from the media playlists and passes them back to your device. So each playlist request your device makes reaches our server with your IP address. We do not store the playlists. The video segments themselves are fetched directly from Twitch's video servers. If live playback falls back to Twitch's own web player, Embr's server serves the page that embeds it, and the web view then loads the player and the stream from Twitch without your access token, so Twitch receives your IP address for that stream. Playback requests carry no Midgar ID and no Twitch token. As with any server, we necessarily see the IP address each request came from. We do not keep IP addresses in our records, except that when you send a report or block, our server keeps your IP address (for an IPv6 address, its /64 network) for up to ten minutes so it can limit how many reports one connection sends. Our rate limits also count requests by address over one-minute windows, to stop abuse. Midgar's servers run on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs for a limited time.

Reports and blocks

When you report or block a chat message or user, the reason, the message's ID, the author's name and ID and, where there is one, the message text and channel are sent to the developer so the report can be reviewed and acted on within 24 hours. Reporting a clip sends its ID, its title, its channel and the name of the person who made it, so it can be reviewed and removed from the feed. These reports do not include your Midgar ID or your Twitch account.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The first time the app starts, it asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret, the date it was created, the date the secret last changed and how the ID was attested. When the app attests, the server also keeps the App Attest public key the app made for the ID, Apple's receipt for it, a request counter and the date the key was last used. It also keeps hashes of the refresh tokens that renew your sign-in, with their dates, and, for an ID merged into another, the ID it was merged into. If you link a Sign in with Apple account through another Midgar app, the server also keeps that account's Apple user identifier and the date it was linked. The app keeps the ID and the secret in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. There is no name, email address, phone number, location or device name in it. When the app creates or joins the ID it may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; we receive Apple's attestation and nothing about you. The ID is used to tie your tips to you and, on the More Apps screen, to show which Midgar apps you already use.

Tips

The optional tips are sold through Apple In-App Purchase and unlock nothing. Apple handles payment and billing, and we never receive your card details or your Apple Account details. When a tip completes, the app sends Midgar's server the signed transaction record from Apple: which tip, the price, the dates, the order identifiers, your App Store country and the Midgar ID. Our server keeps it so Midgar can count tips and keep its financial records, for as long as Apple's financial records require. Apple's own server notifications about a tip also reach the same server, and a tip's purchase and any refund are passed on to Midgar's AI-credits service, which credits only its own credit packs. Until 6 November 2026 our server also passed Apple's notifications on to RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), which keeps what it received under its own privacy policy at revenuecat.com/privacy; those notifications carried the purchase (a tip, Founding Supporter or Studio Pro), the price, the dates, Apple's transaction identifiers, your App Store country and your anonymous Midgar ID, and no name, email address or IP address of yours. The app itself contains no RevenueCat software.

Founding Supporter

Founding Supporter is an optional one-time purchase through Apple In-App Purchase. It unlocks only looks: themes, accent colors, chat fonts, alternate app icons and a flame badge beside your name in chat, in a color you pick, with your name in that color or glowing if you choose. Its purchase record is kept the way a tip's is, described above, and Apple's refund and revocation notices for it reach Midgar's server, which then asks Embr's server to remove the badge. If you are signed in with Twitch, the app also sends Embr's server the signed transaction from Apple and your Twitch access token each time you buy it while signed in and each time you choose Try Again on the supporter screen. It also sends them at launch or when you sign in whenever this device has no record of a claim or the signed-in Twitch account is not the one last claimed on it. That includes a claim another device took away: the app makes it again on its own, so the badge can return without Try Again. The server checks the transaction with Apple's certificates and asks Twitch which account the token belongs to, then stores your Twitch user id with the purchase's original transaction id, whether it was an App Store or a sandbox (test) purchase, the date, your founding number (supporters are numbered in the order they claim; a test purchase has none) and the badge color and name style you pick. When you change the color or style, the app sends them with your Twitch access token again to prove the account. The token itself is not kept. Your Twitch user id, founding number, badge color and name style are then published in the list of supporters that every copy of Embr downloads, so other viewers' apps can show your badge and name; a Twitch user id is already public on Twitch. Claiming the purchase for another Twitch account moves the badge there. A refund or revocation by Apple removes the badge and deletes the record automatically. Otherwise, to remove the badge, email us and we will delete the record.

Studio Pro

Studio Pro is an optional subscription or one-time purchase through Apple In-App Purchase. Its purchase record, including renewals, is kept the way a tip's is: the app and Apple's server notifications send Midgar's server the signed transaction record with your Midgar ID, so Pro is recognised on your other devices and Midgar can keep its financial records. The date your free Pro week started is kept in your device's Keychain and never sent to us.

Embr Studio

Embr Studio, for streamers, shows your own channel or one you moderate. With the permissions you grant at sign-in, the app asks Twitch directly for your channel's events (follows, subscriptions, cheers, raids, channel point redemptions, hype trains, polls, predictions, goals, ad breaks and AutoMod's held messages), its chat and its ad schedule, and sends the actions you choose to Twitch as you. None of this passes through or is kept by Midgar's servers. The stream recap, Studio's settings and your viewer count history stay on your device; your quick messages are kept in your own iCloud account through Apple's iCloud key-value storage so they appear on your other devices, and we never receive them.

More Apps

Settings has a More Apps card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; your App Store country code is sent so prices are correct for your region. It reports which app tiles were displayed, which were tapped, whose App Store page finished loading, and which installed apps were opened from the screen, to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id, your App Store country code, where in the app the screen was opened, and Embr's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total for each day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor numbers only), and for each promoted app or place, so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking. The Midgar ID is sent as a sign-in token only with the separate request that lists which Midgar apps you already use and what you own in them, so the screen does not suggest an app you already have.

The app also sends three more kinds of anonymous count to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app; one count each time the Studio Pro purchase screen opens, with the place it was opened from (for example Settings or a locked Studio feature); and one count for each purchase that names only the product bought. Like the More Apps report, they carry no identifier. They are stored only as running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor numbers only) and, for active counts, the week you first installed the app (for example 2026-W44), with the place or product counted. They are used only to understand how the app is used, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Children

Embr shows live user-generated content and is rated 17+. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your data to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your settings, history and block lists: only on your device, until you delete them or the app. Your favorite channels: also in your own iCloud account, until you remove them.
  • Your Twitch tokens: only in your device's Keychain, until you log out or delete your account.
  • The Midgar ID record and your tip, Founding Supporter and Studio Pro purchase records: until you ask us to delete them, as described below, or as long as Apple's financial records require.
  • Embr Studio's recaps: on your device, for a day after the stream ends.
  • The Founding Supporter badge record (your Twitch user id, the original transaction id, the date, your founding number, badge color and name style): until you ask us to delete it, or until Apple refunds or revokes the purchase.
  • Reports: kept for review, then expire automatically.
  • Apple's records of your purchases, RevenueCat's copy of the purchase notifications it received until 6 November 2026 and Twitch's records of your account: kept by Apple, RevenueCat and Twitch under their own policies.

Deleting your data

In Settings, Delete Account signs you out, revokes Embr's access to your Twitch account and erases Embr's local data on that device. The app does not yet have a button that deletes the Midgar ID. To have your Midgar ID and the tip records linked to it deleted from our server, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc, tell us the app and the approximate date of your first tip, and we will help you do it. We delete the server records, but we cannot change Apple's own records, and we cannot refund a purchase: do that through Apple.

Your rights

Because we keep very little information about you, most requests are answered by the controls above. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us and we will help. We process the Midgar ID and tip records to provide and account for what you buy. Processing takes place in the European Union and the United States, by Cloudflare as our hosting provider, and until 6 November 2026 by RevenueCat. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
