Flaccy Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Flaccy is designed to collect as little as possible. This policy explains what is processed and why. Your playlists and play history stay on your device unless you connect a server (see Servers you connect), and your song files are never sent to us. Only the names, tags and play times of your songs reach us, for AI metadata cleanup, playlists you ask Flaccy to make and Last.fm scrobbling. Midgar's servers see only what the features below need, plus an anonymous Midgar ID, your IP address and the country your connection comes from. There is no advertising in the app and no tracking.

In short

  • Your playlists and play history are stored on your device, and your song files are never uploaded to us. The names, tags and play times of your songs reach us only for AI metadata cleanup, playlists you ask Flaccy to make and Last.fm scrobbling. A server you connect, such as Flaccy Server, receives what you sync to it.
  • The app keeps one anonymous identifier that also works in Midgar's other apps, the Midgar ID. It holds no name or email address.
  • Album and artist lookups and AI metadata cleanup run by themselves when your library loads or is scanned. Last.fm scrobbling, lyric meanings, Make a Playlist, Describe the Sound and the sample album are optional and send data only when you use them.
  • Purchases are made through Apple, and Midgar's purchase ledger keeps the record of them.
  • Nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

Who we are

Flaccy is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. This policy covers Flaccy on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, and Flaccy for Linux, which has its own section below. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

On-device data

Flaccy works without an account: there is no sign-up and no third-party analytics. Your music library, playlists, play history and settings are stored on your device. Your song files, playlists, play history and settings are not uploaded to us, except to a server you connect (see below), and the names, tags and play times of your songs reach us only for AI metadata cleanup, playlists you ask Flaccy to make and Last.fm scrobbling, described below. A diagnostic log is written there too, recording errors and events such as a file name, never the contents of your music or any token. It is not uploaded to us.

Servers you connect

You can connect a server that you run or choose. With Flaccy Server, which you run yourself, the app keeps your library in step with that server. The sync sends each song's title, artist, album, length, audio fingerprint, MusicBrainz id and path relative to your library folder, together with your playlists and their cover images, loved songs, per-song volume settings, the lyric timing you set for a song and play history. With an OpenSubsonic server such as Navidrome, the app streams your music from it and sends your login and the requests for songs and playlists. On Apple devices each server's login is stored in the Keychain, and on Flaccy for Linux it is kept in a file that only your user account can read, in the app's configuration folder. Either way it is sent only to that server. This data goes to a server you run or choose, not to Midgar. On a Mac you can also share your library with your own devices over Tailscale.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The app asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, its sessions, a hash of the secret and the date it was created. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the app creates or joins the ID it may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app. We keep the attested key's identifier and public key, Apple's receipt for the key when Apple includes one, the app's bundle and app identifiers, whether the key came from a production or a development build, a counter of the requests signed with it, and the verification level of the ID (App Attest, development or unverified). The same request starts a session, and the server keeps a hash of its refresh token, a session family id, the app and key it belongs to, and when the token was made, used and expires (30 days). We also keep the times the key was created and last used. We keep nothing else from this step. The ID is sent to Midgar's server at api.midgarcorp.cc, and on the More Apps screen it shows which Midgar apps you already use. Deleting Flaccy does not remove it, because your other Midgar apps share it. To have it deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

Flaccy's server

Six features talk to Flaccy's own server, which the app reaches through Midgar's gateway at api.midgarcorp.cc: AI metadata cleanup, lyric meanings, Make a Playlist, Describe the Sound, Last.fm signing and the sample album. Flaccy's server means Midgar's own service, not a server you run. Flaccy for Linux reaches it directly, as described below. AI metadata cleanup, lyric meanings, Make a Playlist, Describe the Sound and Last.fm signing carry a random identifier that the app generates on your device the first time it needs one. It is not your Midgar ID and holds nothing about you. The server uses it, with your IP address, only to count daily requests so no one can overspend a shared allowance. Each day's counts are removed the next time the server counts a request on a later day. Every request to Midgar's servers arrives with your IP address and the two-letter country Cloudflare finds for your connection. The gateway passes that country on, Flaccy's server does not use it, and the gateway limits how many requests each IP address can make in a short period. Midgar's servers run on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs such as your IP address for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Last.fm (optional)

If you connect a Last.fm account, you authenticate directly with Last.fm; we never see your Last.fm password. While connected, the app sends scrobbles (track, artist, album and timestamp), now-playing updates and loves to Last.fm, and fetches your charts, loved tracks, profile and artist information from it. It also imports your whole scrobble history from Last.fm onto your device and keeps it in sync, where it sits alongside your other play history. Last.fm requires these calls to be signed with a secret that cannot ship inside an app, so for each one the app sends that call's own fields (for a scrobble, the track's details and the time), the app's own Last.fm key and, once you are signed in, your Last.fm session key (which keeps you signed in) to Flaccy's server. The server returns the signature and keeps none of these details. Your Last.fm data is handled under Last.fm's privacy policy. You can disconnect at any time in Settings.

AI metadata cleanup

AI metadata cleanup runs by itself. When a library loads or is scanned, the app sends each local song it has not yet checked, for as long as the app allows playback. Playback stops being allowed when a free trial ends without a purchase. For each such song, the song's path inside your library (folder and file names) and the artist, album, title and track number in its tags are sent over an encrypted connection to Flaccy's server. The server forwards them to Anthropic's Claude model, where it identifies the artist, album, and track and cleans up the names. Audio content is never sent. Apart from a random identifier kept on your device for daily limits and the IP address described above, the request carries no identifier of yours. The text is processed only to return the result, and our server does not keep it. You can also start a new analysis yourself in Settings.

Lyric meanings (optional)

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, when you open the meaning of a song or of one line, the app sends the song's title, artist and album, its sung lyrics and any Wikipedia background it found to Flaccy's server. The server forwards them to Anthropic's Claude model for the reading and keeps none of the text. The reading is stored on your device. Like AI metadata cleanup, it works only while playback is allowed (a free trial or Flaccy Pro), it shares that allowance, and the request carries no identifier of yours apart from the random identifier and IP address described above. Flaccy for Linux does not use this service: it asks a language model on your own computer instead.

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, when you make a playlist from a sentence, the app sends your sentence and, for each artist in your library, the artist's name, genre and years and how many songs and loved songs of theirs you have. It then sends the title, artist, album, year, length and loved mark of up to 300 songs the playlist could hold. Flaccy's server forwards them to Anthropic's Claude model, which chooses among them, and keeps none of the text. File names, folders, play counts and listening times are not sent. When you describe a sound in the equalizer, the app sends your sentence, whether you are on a phone, a tablet or a computer, the kind of output you are listening on (such as headphones or a car, never its name) and the equalizer curve in use, and the model answers with a curve. Like lyric meanings, both work only while playback is allowed, share the daily allowance and carry no identifier of yours apart from the random identifier and IP address described above. Flaccy for Linux has neither feature.

Sample album

The optional sample album is a public-domain recording that the app downloads from Flaccy's server when you ask for it. Apart from the IP address described above, the download carries no identifier of yours.

Purchases

Flaccy Pro and the lifetime unlock are sold through Apple In-App Purchase. Each purchase carries your anonymous Midgar ID as Apple's app account token. The app sends Apple's signed transaction record to Midgar's purchase ledger at api.midgarcorp.cc. The ledger keeps the product, the price and currency, the App Store country where you bought it, the purchase and expiry dates, Apple's transaction identifiers, the app's bundle identifier, the kind of offer used, whether you bought it or your family shared it, whether it came from the sandbox or the production store, and the renewal, refund and revocation details that Apple reports. The ledger links these to your Midgar ID so that Flaccy Pro is recognised on all your devices and your purchases can be restored in your other Midgar apps. Apple's own server notifications about your purchases reach the same ledger. Neither the app nor our servers send anything about your purchases to RevenueCat now. Until 6 November 2026 our server also passed Apple's notifications about your purchases on to RevenueCat, which keeps what it received under its own privacy policy. We never receive your payment-card details.

Other service lookups

To enrich your library, the app looks up album and artist details, cover art, artist images and lyrics on its own as your library loads or is scanned. For each album that still lacks details, it sends the album and artist names to Last.fm and, while details or artwork are missing, to MusicBrainz, and to Deezer while the cover is missing. It downloads cover images from the address Last.fm returns, the Cover Art Archive, Deezer and Apple Music. For each artist, it sends the artist name to Last.fm and Deezer. The Last.fm lookups use the app's own Last.fm key, and when you have connected Last.fm, your Last.fm user name is sent with album lookups so Last.fm can return your play counts. Lyrics come from LRCLIB, which receives the track and artist names, the album name when it is known and the track's length when it is known. Apple Music, through Apple's MusicKit after you allow Apple Music access, is also used for artwork and artist images, and to find the song or album that a share link is made for. Share links are made by song.link or album.link, which receive only the Apple Music id of that song or album. The Wantlist's new-release check, when it runs, sends the names of some of your library's artists and of artists on your wantlist to Apple's iTunes Search. Tapping a preview in the Wantlist sends that track's title and artist to Apple's iTunes Search to find its preview clip. The Meaning sheet asks Wikipedia about a song only when you open it. When you search for artwork yourself, the app also searches Apple's iTunes Search, MusicBrainz and Deezer, and the Cover Art Archive. Apart from your Last.fm user name, these requests carry no identifier of yours.

More Apps

Settings has a Midgar universe card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; your App Store country code is sent so prices are correct for your region. It reports which app tiles were displayed and which were tapped to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id, your App Store country code, where in the app the screen was opened, and the app's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total per day, event, app, platform, app version (major and minor) and place in the app, so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking. The Midgar ID is sent as a sign-in token with the request that lists which Midgar apps you already use and with the purchase and entitlement requests to the ledger. The usage counts and the More Apps report never carry it.

The app also sends further anonymous counts to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app; one count for each purchase that names only the product bought; and counts of the steps on the way to buying Pro, namely first-run steps (such as playing the sample album, adding or importing music, or starting the free trial), the paywall being shown, and the outcome of a checkout (started, pending, cancelled or failed) with the plan chosen. Like the More Apps report, they carry no identifier. The server stores running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor), and for the active count and the steps toward Pro also the install week, plus a fixed category from a closed list, such as a screen or a step. It never keeps a per-person or per-device record. They are used only to understand how the app is used and what stops people from buying, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Flaccy for Linux

Flaccy for Linux comes from the same project and is distributed from GitHub and the AUR. It does not use your Midgar ID or AI metadata cleanup, and it has connections of its own. The sections above on Last.fm and Servers you connect apply to it too. Music videos are always on: the app searches YouTube for each song's official video when you play it and streams it to your computer. It also prepares videos for the upcoming queue in the background and keeps them on disk up to a size cap, and the Prepare Music Videos setting turns that off. Comments under the video come from YouTube, and Downloads fetch the audio of any link you paste that yt-dlp supports, such as YouTube, SoundCloud and Bandcamp links. These services receive the request from your computer, so they see your IP address and what you search for or play. The Linux app also uses the lookup services listed above (Last.fm, MusicBrainz, the Cover Art Archive, Deezer, LRCLIB, Apple's iTunes Search and song.link), and Wikipedia for the meaning of a song. Its calls to Flaccy's server, for Last.fm signing and the sample album, go directly to flaccy-api.midgarcorp.cc. Lyric meanings and the choice of music video can come from a language model that you run yourself. That model runs on your own computer or network, not on Midgar's servers.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your data to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your library, playlists, play history, the log and the app's own crash reports: only on your device, until you delete the app. A server you connect keeps what is synced to it. When you choose Contact Support, the email draft has the log and the latest crash reports attached so the problem can be found; you see them before sending and can remove them, and they reach us only if you send the email.
  • The request counters of Flaccy's server: removed the next time the server counts a request on a later day.
  • The Midgar ID record: until you ask us to delete it. An ID whose verification level is unverified is deleted automatically once it is more than 30 days old, has no Apple sign-in link and has no session started or renewed in the last 30 days.
  • Keychain items on Apple devices (your Last.fm session, your server logins and device token, your trial start date, your Midgar ID and your Midgar ID session on this device, which holds a refresh token): deleting the app does not remove them. On Flaccy for Linux, the same Last.fm session, server logins and device token sit in files in its configuration folder, which removing the app does not delete. Disconnecting Last.fm or removing a server in Settings deletes that item. Your Midgar ID is removed when you ask us to delete it. An ID whose verification level is unverified is deleted automatically once it is more than 30 days old, has no Apple sign-in link and has no session started or renewed in the last 30 days.
  • Purchase records: Midgar's purchase ledger keeps them as financial records, linked to your Midgar ID until that ID is deleted, after which nothing links them to a live ID. RevenueCat keeps the records it received from earlier versions of the app, and from our server until 6 November 2026, under its own policy. Apple's records of your purchases: kept by Apple.

Your choices and rights

You can delete the app at any time to remove its library, playlists, play history and settings from the device. Keychain items and, on Flaccy for Linux, the files holding the same details stay after deletion, so disconnect Last.fm and remove each server in Settings before you delete the app if you want them gone, and ask us to delete your Midgar ID if you want that gone too. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information to provide the features and purchases you ask for. Processing by Cloudflare, our hosting provider, takes place at whichever of its locations serves a request, which may be anywhere in the world, and processing by Anthropic, our AI provider, takes place wherever Anthropic runs its services. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
