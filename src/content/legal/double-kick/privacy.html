Double Kick Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

Double Kick turns a photo of a menu into a translated, explained list of dishes and drinks. It has no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking. Your scan history and taste preferences stay on your device. What reaches Midgar's servers is the photo the AI needs to read the menu, an anonymous identity that keeps your credit balance, an anonymous Midgar ID, the Apple device checks described under Identity and The anonymous Midgar ID, anonymous usage counts with no identifier, and what is needed to recognise your purchases.

In short

  • The menu photo you scan is sent to our server and on to Google Gemini to read and translate the items. It is used only to make your result.
  • The app keeps anonymous identifiers: an account key for your credit balance, a random device identifier that the account is tied to, and a Midgar ID that also works in Midgar's other apps. None of them holds a name, email address or other detail about you, unless you sign in with Apple in another Midgar app, which links your Midgar ID to your Apple account.
  • Credit packs are bought through Apple. Our own server verifies them and keeps the record of what you bought.
  • Your scan history, favorites and taste preferences are stored on your device and are not uploaded to us, except that a scan's location, when its photo carries one, is sent to Apple to name the place (see On-device data).
  • The app does not ask for your location, but it reads the location tag a photo already carries, if it has one. Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

Who we are

Double Kick is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

AI processing

To provide the app's core feature, the photo of the menu you scan, taken with the camera or chosen from your library, is sent over an encrypted connection to our server at api.midgarcorp.cc, which passes it to Google Gemini together with our own instructions to read and translate the items. The photo is processed only to produce your result and is not used to train AI models. Our AI provider handles the request under its own privacy and security commitments and is permitted to use it only to return your result. Our server does not keep your photo or the translated result. It keeps a record of each scan's cost (when it happened and how many credits it used) against your anonymous account, which is deleted with the account. A scan that carries your Midgar ID's access token, which the app sends when the token is ready within two seconds, is linked to that ID, and its cost is recorded against the ID instead. The photo is re-encoded before it is sent, so location and other camera metadata are not included.

Identity

Double Kick works without a sign-up. On first launch we create an anonymous, device-based account used to hold your credit balance. To do this, the app asks Apple's DeviceCheck service for a token that shows the request comes from a genuine Apple device, and it sends that token with a random device identifier that stays in the app's keychain. Our server checks the token with Apple, does not keep it, and keeps the identifier with the account. The account holds no name, email address or phone number. The app does not offer Sign in with Apple. When you delete your account in Profile, this account, your credit balance and the records linked to it are deleted from our server.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The app also asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret, the date it was created, the app it was made for and how the device proved itself. Refresh tokens are kept only as hashes. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the ID is created or joined the app may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; we receive Apple's attestation and nothing about you. For a device that uses App Attest, the server keeps the public key Apple's attestation gave for the app's key on that device, and the receipt Apple issued for that key when it sent one. If you sign in with Apple in another Midgar app, your Midgar ID is linked to your Apple account. The ID's access token is sent with each scan when the app has it within two seconds, with your purchases, and with the request that shows, on the Midgar universe screen, which Midgar apps you already use. It is not removed by Delete Account in Double Kick, because your other Midgar apps share it. To have it deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

Purchases

Credit packs are sold through Apple In-App Purchase. Purchases carry your anonymous Midgar ID as Apple's app account token whenever the app can read that ID when the purchase starts. If it cannot, the purchase still goes through without the token, and the ledger links it to your Midgar ID the first time the app reports it. The app sends the signed transaction record Apple issues to Midgar's purchase ledger at api.midgarcorp.cc. The ledger keeps which pack, the price and currency, the App Store country of the purchase, the dates, whether the purchase was made in production or sandbox, and Apple's transaction identifiers, linked to your Midgar ID so that your purchases can be restored in your other Midgar apps. Apple's own server notifications about your purchases reach the same ledger. Our server checks the signed transaction before it credits your balance. From version 1.1.9, neither the app nor our servers send anything about your purchases to RevenueCat; earlier versions have RevenueCat validate purchases, and it receives the purchase receipt from Apple, an anonymous identifier that it generates and your IP address, but not your name or email address. We never receive your payment-card details.

On-device data

Content you create in the app, such as the translated menus in your history, your favorites and your taste preferences, is stored on your device and is not uploaded to us. The menu photo itself is not kept in the history. When a scan's photo carries a location tag, the app keeps its coordinates with the scan and sends them to Apple, which returns a place name for the history; our server never receives them. If you take a photo with the camera and allow it, the app saves that photo to your Photos library. A diagnostic log is written on the device too. It records the app's routine events and errors, never the contents of a menu or any token. The app has no way to send it anywhere, so it leaves your device only if someone copies it with developer tools connected to the phone.

More Apps

Profile has a Midgar universe card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; the region set in your device's settings is sent so prices are correct for that region. When you tap an app you do not have that runs on this device, its App Store page opens in a sheet inside Double Kick, and Apple receives a campaign label that names Double Kick and the Profile screen, together with Midgar's App Store provider code, so that installs can be counted. If that page cannot load, the App Store app opens instead and no label is sent. It reports which app tiles were displayed and which were tapped, when the Midgar universe screen is opened, when an app's App Store page finishes loading, and when an installed app is opened from it, to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id (where the event has one), the region set in your device's settings, where in the app the screen was opened, and the app's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total per day, event, app and platform, so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking.

The app also sends three more kinds of anonymous count to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app, one count each time the credit store is opened, naming only where it was opened, and one count for each purchase that names only the product bought. Like the More Apps report, they carry no identifier and are stored only as running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor number only), with the install week for active counts. They are used only to understand how the app is used, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar's server runs on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs such as your IP address for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. It contains no advertising and no user-to-user communication.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising inside the app, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your data to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your scan history, favorites, taste preferences and the log: only on your device, until you delete the app or your account.
  • Your anonymous account, its credit balance and the cost records kept against it: until you delete your account in Profile.
  • The Midgar ID record: until you ask us to delete it, except that an ID made without Apple's App Attest check is deleted once the app has gone 30 days without renewing its session, and any credits it holds go with it. Cost records of Double Kick scans made with your Midgar ID: until you delete your account in Profile, which erases them.
  • Purchase records: Midgar's purchase ledger keeps them as financial records, linked to your Midgar ID until that ID is deleted, after which nothing links them to a live ID. RevenueCat keeps the records it received from earlier versions of the app under its own policy. Apple's records of your purchases: kept by Apple.

Your choices and rights

You can delete your account in Profile at any time, which removes your anonymous account, its credit balance, and the history, favorites, taste preferences and diagnostic log saved on the device. It does not remove your Midgar ID or the device identifier kept in your keychain, as described in Identity and The anonymous Midgar ID; the device identifier is reused when the app next creates an account. Deleting the app alone removes the on-device data but not the server-side account. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information to provide the translations and purchases you ask for. Processing takes place in the European Union and the United States, by Cloudflare as our hosting provider and by Google as our AI provider. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
