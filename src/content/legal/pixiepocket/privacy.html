PixiePocket Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

PixiePocket turns a photo or a description into an image with AI. It has no advertising and no tracking. Your results are kept in a private gallery, which only your account can browse unless you make an image public. What reaches Midgar's servers is what the AI needs to make an image, the images it made, an account that keeps your credit balance and gallery, an anonymous Midgar ID, a DeviceCheck token for the device, a random device identifier, anonymous usage counts, and what is needed to recognise your purchases.

In short

  • The prompts you write and the photos you attach are sent to our server and on to Google Gemini or OpenAI, and sometimes both, to make your image. They are used only to make your result.
  • If you turn on photo ideas, a small copy of a photo you pick is sent to our server and on to Anthropic, whose Claude model suggests three edits for it. Nothing is sent for ideas until you agree, and you can turn them off in Settings.
  • The images you make are saved to your private gallery on our servers, with the prompt that made each one, so they appear on every device you sign in on. They are private unless you make one public, and a public image can be listed, with its prompt, to anyone without signing in. Each image also has a web address that opens it without signing in, so anyone who has that address can see the image. You can delete any image, or your whole account, and they are deleted.
  • Our server keeps a usage record of each request, which includes the text of your prompt. It does not keep the photos you attach.
  • The app works without sign-up on an anonymous account. If you choose to sign in with Google or GitHub, we also keep the name and email address that provider shares with us. With Sign in with Apple, we keep the email address Apple shares with us, or a placeholder when it shares none, and never your name.
  • Purchases are made through Apple. Our own server verifies them and keeps the record of what you bought.
  • Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

Who we are

PixiePocket is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

AI processing

To provide the app's core feature, the text prompts you enter, and any photos you provide for editing, are sent over an encrypted connection to our server at api.midgarcorp.cc, which passes them to Google Gemini or OpenAI, depending on the model you choose, to generate or edit an image. Whichever model you choose, the same prompt and photos may be sent to the other provider if the first one fails or declines the request. Every request is checked by automated moderation. The prompts and photos are processed only to produce your result and are not used to train AI models. Our AI providers handle them under their own privacy and security commitments that protect them to a standard comparable to this policy, and are permitted to use them only to return your result. You approve this before your first image and can withdraw your approval in Settings.

Photo ideas are a separate choice. When you pick a photo, the app can suggest three edits that fit it. This happens only after you agree in the app, with the Suggest 3 Edits button under your photo or the Photo Ideas switch in Settings, and you can switch it off in Settings at any time. A copy of the photo, reduced to 512 pixels on its longest side, and the name of the language the app is shown in are sent over an encrypted connection to our server, which passes them to Anthropic, whose Claude model writes the suggestions. Our server keeps neither the photo nor the suggestions, only a record that a request was made. Anthropic processes the copy only to answer and does not use it to train AI models.

Our server keeps a usage record of each request (when, what it cost, which model answered and the text of your prompt). It does not keep the photos you attach. The record is linked to your account and is deleted with it.

Your images

Each image you generate or edit is stored on our servers, in storage run by Cloudflare, in your private gallery, together with the prompt that made it. It is linked to your account, and nobody else can open it unless you share it yourself from your device. Each image has a long random web address that opens it without signing in. The address is shown only to you while the image is private, but anyone who has the address can open the image. It stays there until you delete it in the app or delete your account. A small copy is also kept in the app's cache on your device.

Accounts and identity

On first launch we create an anonymous, device-based account that holds your credit balance and your gallery. It holds no name, email address or phone number. To create it, the app sends a DeviceCheck token for the device to our server, which checks it with Apple's DeviceCheck service. The app also keeps a random device identifier in your Keychain and sends it to our server, which keeps it with your anonymous account until you delete the account. The app keeps its key in your device's Keychain.

You may also sign in with Apple, Google or GitHub, for example to keep your images and credits when you change phone. We then receive the identifier that provider gives us and link it to your account. With Google or GitHub, we also receive your name and your email address where the provider shares them. With Sign in with Apple, we receive the email address Apple shares with us, which may be a private relay address, or a placeholder address when Apple shares none. We do not receive your name from Apple, so your account shows the name Apple User. We use these details only to recognise your account. When you delete your account in Settings, this account, your credit balance, your gallery and the records linked to it are deleted from our server.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The app also asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret and the date it was created. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the app creates or joins the ID it may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; for that key we keep its identifier, its public key, Apple's attestation receipt, the app's bundle identifier, the app's registry identifier, the environment the key was made in, the time it was made, a use counter and the time the key was last used, all linked to the ID, and we keep nothing else about you for it. The ID is sent as a sign-in token with the app's requests to our server whenever the app can read it, and with the requests to our purchase ledger, so that those requests can be linked to your account. On the More Apps screen it is also sent to read which Midgar apps you already use. It is not removed by Delete Account in PixiePocket, because your other Midgar apps share it. To have it deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

Purchases

Credit packs and PixiePocket Plus, a weekly subscription that renews until you cancel it, are sold through Apple In-App Purchase. Each purchase carries your anonymous Midgar ID as Apple's app account token. The app sends the signed transaction record Apple issues (which pack or subscription, the price, the dates and Apple's transaction identifiers) to Midgar's purchase ledger at api.midgarcorp.cc, which keeps it linked to your Midgar ID and records whether Plus is active, so that your purchases can be restored in your other Midgar apps, and Apple's own server notifications about your purchases reach the same ledger. Our server checks the signed transaction before it credits your balance or grants Plus. From version 1.7.0, neither the app nor our servers send anything about your purchases to RevenueCat; earlier versions have RevenueCat validate purchases, and it receives the purchase receipt from Apple, an identifier that is your PixiePocket account id and your IP address, but not your name or email address. We never receive your payment-card details.

On-device data

The app keeps a cache of recent images, your settings and a diagnostic log on your device. The log records errors and what the app did, such as a failed purchase or a deleted account, each with a time, a level and a category. It never records your prompts or any token. It leaves your device only if you choose to share it. Deleting the app removes this data from the device but not your account on our server.

More Apps

Settings has a Midgar universe card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; your App Store country code is sent so prices are correct for your region. It reports when the More Apps screen is opened, and for each app tile whether it was displayed, tapped, finished loading or opened, to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id, your App Store country code, where in the app the screen was opened, and the app's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total per day, event, app and platform, so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking.

The app also sends three more kinds of anonymous count to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app; one count each time the credit or Plus store is shown, with the place it was opened from; and one count for each purchase that names only the product bought. They carry no identifier and are stored only as running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor number), together with the place or product counted. The active count is also stored with the week the app was first installed. They are used only to understand how the app is used, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar's server runs on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs such as your IP address for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. It contains no advertising and no user-to-user communication.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising inside the app, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your prompts, photos or images to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your images and the prompt that made each: in your private gallery until you delete them or your account.
  • Your account, its device identifier, credit balance, usage records (including prompt text), records of each time a purchase was needed with your balance at that moment, and the date your first edit started Pixie Week: until you delete your account in Settings.
  • Name and email address from a Google or GitHub sign-in, and the email address from Sign in with Apple: until you delete your account.
  • The Midgar ID record and its App Attest key record: until you ask us to delete it. An ID that was never attested and has no Apple sign-in is deleted automatically once it has had no session for 30 days.
  • The cache and the log: only on your device, until you clear the cache or delete the app.
  • Purchase records: Midgar's purchase ledger keeps them as financial records, linked to your Midgar ID until that ID is deleted, after which nothing links them to a live ID. RevenueCat keeps the records it received from earlier versions of the app under its own policy. Apple's records of your purchases: kept by Apple.

Your choices and rights

You can withdraw your approval of cloud AI, turn photo ideas off, delete individual images, or delete your account in Settings at any time. The anonymous counts are on in the app. The app has no setting to switch them off. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information to provide the images and purchases you ask for. Processing takes place in the European Union and the United States, by Cloudflare as our hosting provider and by Google and OpenAI as our AI providers. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
