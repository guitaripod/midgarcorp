Dream Eater Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Dream Eater is a private dream journal. This policy explains what stays on your device, what leaves it, who handles it and how to delete it.

In short

  • Your dreams, voice recordings, journal, birth details and sky data stay on your device, except what is sent for a reading, a lens, a follow-up question, a picture or a month's letter (see below), and the birth place you type, which goes to Apple's place search. Unless you turn that off in Settings, a picture is made after a standard reading while you have Plus or a credit to spend, and a short scene summary and a few mood words leave your device for it.
  • The app keeps two anonymous identifiers, an account key for your credit balance and a Midgar ID that also works in Midgar's other apps. Neither holds your name or email address.
  • Requests to Midgar's servers for readings, art, credits and your Midgar ID go through Midgar's gateway at api.midgarcorp.cc.
  • Nothing is sold, and there is no advertising inside the app and no tracking.

Who we are

Dream Eater is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

What stays on your device

Your dreams, voice recordings, transcripts, journal, tags, birth details and the sky data worked out from them are stored on your device. The parts that leave it are described in the sections below, and your birth date, time and place are never sent to us. Speech is turned into text on your device. Your dreams, recordings, journal and birth details are also included in any backup of your iPhone, to iCloud or a computer, under Apple's own policy. A diagnostic log is written there too, recording events and errors such as counts and error codes, never the text of a dream, a title, a reading or your birth details. The log is left out of those backups. It is not uploaded to us unless you choose to send it with a message to support, after the app has shown you exactly what goes with it: the app and iOS versions, device model, language and region, whether you have Plus, your credit balance, the state of notifications and microphone access, the journal migration status and the last 200 lines of the log. It never includes dream text, titles, readings or birth details.

When an update changes how the journal is stored, the app copies your journal into the new storage and keeps the old copy on your device for 30 days, in case the update needs to be undone, and then removes it. Delete all data removes it at once.

Readings

Only when you ask for a reading, the dream text, the tone, feelings, flags and other options you chose for it, the people, places and intention you added, and, while Use earlier dreams to find threads is on (it is on by default), the identifier, title, date, tags and short summary of up to eight earlier dreams you had read, are sent to Dream Eater's server through Midgar's gateway at api.midgarcorp.cc, which passes them to Google's Gemini model to write the reading. Any lens, and a follow-up question, sends the same dream details as a reading and the reading it belongs to. Asking for the sky lens also sends the sky recorded with the dream and the signs of your chart (sun, moon and rising, each where the app knows it). The sky is the time of the dream, the Moon's sign, degree, phase, illumination and whether it is near a sign boundary, any new, first quarter, full or last quarter moon that night with whether it is at perigee and any eclipse, the retrograde and stationary planets, up to three aspects and up to three transits. A follow-up question also sends your question and the earlier turns of that conversation.

Our server keeps no dream text, prompt or reading afterwards: it passes the request on, returns the answer to your device and forgets it. You can turn off earlier dreams in Settings and withdraw your permission for readings at any time in Settings, then Privacy and security, and everything else keeps working.

Art

Art is made from a short scene summary and a few mood words, never from your dream text. Unless you turn off Include art by default in Settings, a picture is made after each standard reading you get while you have Plus or a credit to spend, unless the reading raises a safety concern. The summary and mood words go through our server to Google's image model. The picture is returned to your device and is not stored on our server.

The month's letter

Once a calendar month has eight logged nights, you can ask for a letter about that month. Only when you ask, and after you have agreed once to who writes it, the app sends the month's counts (nights logged, dreams, nights with no dream remembered and lucid dreams), how often each tone, feeling and tag appeared, and one sentence from each reading of that month that raised no safety concern, to Dream Eater's server through Midgar's gateway at api.midgarcorp.cc, which passes them to Anthropic's Claude model to write the letter. The text of your dreams, their titles and the people and places you added are not sent. The letter is returned to your device and kept in your journal, and the server keeps none of it. Withdrawing your permission for readings in Settings withdraws this agreement too.

Identity

Dream Eater works without an account. On first launch we create an anonymous account used to hold your credit balance and to recognise your subscription. It holds no name, email address or phone number. To create it, the app sends a token from Apple's DeviceCheck service, which our server checks with Apple, and a random device identifier that the app keeps in your keychain so that it survives reinstalling. Our server keeps that identifier with the account, but not the token.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The app also asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret, and the dates it was created and its secret last changed. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the app creates or joins the ID it may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; we keep the attested key's public key, Apple's attestation receipt and a count of the requests it has signed, with the ID. The ID is sent with requests to our server as a sign-in token, so that our server can link it to your anonymous account, and on the More Apps screen it shows which Midgar apps you already use. It is not removed by Delete all data in Dream Eater, because your other Midgar apps share it. To have it deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

What our server keeps

  • An anonymous identifier and key for your device. There is no name and no email address.
  • The Midgar ID, a hash of its secret, the dates it was created and its secret last changed.
  • For each App Attest key attached to the Midgar ID, the key's public key, Apple's attestation receipt where Apple gave one, how many requests the key has signed and when it was last used.
  • Hashed refresh tokens for the sessions of the Midgar ID.
  • Your credit balance and the ledger of credits granted, used and bought.
  • Counters for your weekly free readings.
  • Apple's attribution verdict for your install: whether it came from one of our ads and, if it did, the campaign details (see Advertising measurement below).
  • A SHA-256 fingerprint of the attribution token, so that the same token is not counted twice. The token itself is never stored.

Purchases

Credit packs and the optional subscription are sold through Apple In-App Purchase. Each purchase carries your anonymous Midgar ID as Apple's app account token. The app sends the signed transaction record Apple issues (which product, the price, the dates and Apple's transaction identifiers) to Midgar's purchase ledger at api.midgarcorp.cc, which keeps it linked to your Midgar ID so that your subscription is recognised on all your devices and your purchases can be restored in your other Midgar apps, and our server checks the signed transaction of a credit pack before it credits your balance. Apple also sends its own server notifications about your purchases to the same ledger, and they go no further than two places: for a credit pack purchase or a refund, they go on to our credit service with your Midgar ID, which credits or reverses the pack; and for each purchase, renewal, refund or revocation, a sales alert carrying the product, price, currency, storefront, environment and date goes to our sales bot, never with your Midgar ID. Until 6 November 2026 the ledger also passed them on to RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), which keeps what it received under its own privacy policy at revenuecat.com/privacy; those notifications carried the product, the price, the dates, Apple's transaction identifiers, your App Store country and your anonymous Midgar ID, and no name, email address or IP address of yours. From version 2.0.2 the app itself contains no RevenueCat software; earlier versions process purchases with RevenueCat's software in the app, which receives the purchase receipt from Apple, an anonymous identifier and your IP address. We never receive your payment-card details.

Who handles data

  • Google (Gemini): writes readings and paints art from the text described above.
  • Anthropic (Claude): writes the month's letter from the counts, tags, feelings and reading sentences described above.
  • RevenueCat: processes purchases in versions before 2.0.2, and received Apple's server notifications about your purchases from Midgar's purchase ledger until 6 November 2026.
  • Apple: StoreKit for purchases, DeviceCheck and App Attest checks, Apple Ads attribution that tells us which ad campaign an install came from, and MapKit place search.
  • Cloudflare: hosts our servers and the gateway. Connecting to them gives Cloudflare your IP address, which reaches our gateway and services. Our gateway and services use that address only to limit how often a network can make requests. Cloudflare gives the gateway the two-letter country of each connection, which it passes to our services with each request. Cloudflare may keep standard request logs, such as your IP address, for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Google handles the text sent for a reading or for art under its own terms and privacy policy. Under its terms for the paid Gemini API, Google does not use those prompts and responses to improve its products, and it keeps a log of them for a limited time solely to detect abuse. Anthropic handles what is sent for a month's letter under its own terms and privacy policy, and under its commercial terms it may not train its models on it. We do not use your dreams to train AI models, and nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

Apple's MapKit search receives the place you type for your birth details, to suggest and look up that place. If you saved a place while offline, the app sends the same text again each time it opens until Apple finds it. Your birth date and time are not part of that search.

More Apps

Settings has a Midgar card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; your App Store country code is sent so prices are correct for your region. It asks your device, without any network call, which of Midgar's other apps are installed, so that it does not suggest an app you already have; that list is not sent anywhere. With your Midgar ID as the sign-in token it asks our identity service and ledger which Midgar apps you use and what you own in them, to show them under Your Midgar; this only displays information and grants nothing. It reports which app tiles were displayed and which were tapped to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id, your App Store country code, where in the app the screen was opened, and the app's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total per day, event, app, platform and app version (major.minor), so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking.

The app also sends further anonymous counts to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app; one count for each purchase that names only the product bought; and counts of the steps on the way to buying, namely the Plus screen being shown, with where in the app it was opened from, and the outcome of a checkout (started, pending, cancelled or failed) with the plan or credit pack chosen. Like the More Apps report, they carry no identifier and are stored only as running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major.minor), with the install's ISO week added for active installs and funnel steps, and the product, placement, outcome or step each count names. They are used only to understand how the app is used and what stops people from buying, never for advertising or tracking.

Advertising measurement

We may promote Dream Eater with ads on the App Store. After you install the app, it sends Apple's attribution token to our server, and keeps sending it on later launches until Apple's answer is final, or until five launches have reached our server without one. Our server then asks Apple whether the install came from one of those ads. If it did, we keep Apple's numeric campaign, ad group, keyword and ad IDs and the details Apple returns, including the App Store country it was served in, but never who you are. If it did not, we keep that verdict along with the details Apple returns. Apple's App Store attribution includes no advertising identifier and no device identifier, which is why it requires no tracking permission. We keep those campaign details alongside your anonymous identity purely to see which ads pay for themselves, we receive nothing that could recognise you in any other app or website, and we never share it.

Deleting your data

In the app, open Settings, then Privacy and security, then Delete all data. This removes the dreams and everything else stored on your device, including the copy of your journal an earlier version kept, except the diagnostic log, which it does not clear (see How long we keep data), and deletes your server identity, credit ledger and weekly counters. It does not remove the Midgar ID, which Midgar's other apps share; email marcus@midgarcorp.cc to have it deleted. Copies in an iPhone backup made before you use it stay in that backup until you delete the backup. It cannot be undone, and it does not cancel a subscription: manage or cancel that in iOS Settings under your Apple Account, then Subscriptions. Purchase records held by Apple and RevenueCat are kept under their own policies.

How long we keep data

  • Your dreams, recordings, journal and birth details: on your device until you delete the app or use Delete all data, and in any iPhone backup until you delete that backup.
  • The log: only on your device, until newer lines push it out (the current file and three older copies, each up to 2 MB) or you delete the app. Delete all data does not clear it.
  • Your anonymous account, its credit balance and usage records: until you use Delete all data.
  • The Midgar ID record: until you ask us to delete it.
  • Purchase records: Midgar's purchase ledger keeps them as financial records, linked to your Midgar ID until that ID is deleted, after which nothing links them to a live ID. RevenueCat's records of your purchases, including the notifications it received until 6 November 2026: kept by RevenueCat under its own policy. Apple's records of your purchases: kept by Apple.

Your rights

Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information to provide the readings, art, credits and purchases you ask for. Processing takes place where Cloudflare's global network runs our servers, by Google as our AI provider and by RevenueCat. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).

Children

Dream Eater is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising inside the app, or use third-party tracking or advertising identifiers.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Changes
