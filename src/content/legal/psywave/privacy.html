Psywave Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Psywave turns a description or a photo into an Apple Music playlist. It has no account to create, no third-party advertising and no tracking. Your playlists stay on your device unless you share one or build on one with the AI. What reaches Midgar's servers is what the AI needs to make a playlist, an anonymous identity that keeps your credit balance, an anonymous Midgar ID, what is needed to recognise your purchases, a playlist you choose to share with Share Link, and the anonymous usage counts described below.

In short

  • The description or photo you give is sent to our server and on to Anthropic (or, if our settings cannot be read, to Google Gemini) to write the playlist. It is used only to make your result.
  • If you ask for a listening letter (Psywave Pro), the names, captions, moods and genres of up to twelve of your saved playlists and up to eight songs of each are sent the same way to Anthropic to write it.
  • The app keeps anonymous identifiers: an account key for your credit balance, a random identifier kept in the device's Keychain for that account, and a Midgar ID that also works in Midgar's other apps. Except for an Apple account link you make in another Midgar app that offers Sign in with Apple, none of them holds a name, email address or other detail about you.
  • Purchases are made through Apple. Our own server verifies them and keeps the record of what you bought.
  • Your playlist history is stored on your device. A playlist is stored on our server only when you tap Share Link. Remixing a playlist, adding songs to one or growing one of your Apple Music playlists sends it to our AI provider, as described under AI processing and Apple Music.
  • Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

Who we are

Psywave is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

AI processing

To provide the app's core feature, the description or photo you provide is sent over an encrypted connection to our server at api.midgarcorp.cc, which passes it to Anthropic to generate a playlist (or, if our settings cannot be read, to Google Gemini). Every request in this section also carries your Apple Music country and your discovery setting, so the suggested songs match the storefront you listen in, and every one except adding songs to a playlist also carries your device's language. Remixing a playlist you saved or adding songs to one sends that playlist's name, description and song titles and artists, and growing one of your Apple Music playlists sends its name and song titles and artists, so the AI can suggest songs that fit. The photo and the description are processed only to produce your result and are not used to train AI models. Our AI provider handles the request under its own privacy and security commitments. Our server keeps a record of each playlist request you make: when it was made and how many credits it cost. The record holds neither the text of your description, nor your photo, nor the name of the AI model. It is linked to your anonymous account and is deleted with it.

The listening letter is written only when you ask for it. For a letter, the names, captions, moods and genres of up to twelve of your saved playlists, and the title and artist of up to eight songs of each, are sent over the same connection to our server, which passes them to Anthropic to write the letter and the playlist it suggests. Nothing else from your history is sent, the letter is returned to your device and kept there, and the server keeps none of it beyond the usage record described here.

Identity

Psywave works without a sign-up. On first launch we create an anonymous, device-based account used to hold your credit balance and to recognise your Psywave Pro subscription. To create it, the app sends a random identifier kept in the device's Keychain and a token from Apple's DeviceCheck service. Our server checks that token with Apple to confirm the request comes from a genuine device, and keeps the identifier with the account. The account holds no name, email address or phone number. The app does not offer Sign in with Apple. When you delete your account in Settings, this account, your credit balance and the records linked to it are deleted from our server.

The anonymous Midgar ID

The app also asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret, the date it was created and, when App Attest was used, the key the app registered for it: its public key, Apple's attestation receipt, the app's bundle ID and whether the build is a production or development build. It also keeps how the ID was verified (App Attest, a development build or not verified), when its secret was last rotated, whether it has been merged into another ID, and hashed refresh tokens with their dates. If you link this Midgar ID to your Apple account with Sign in with Apple in one of Midgar's apps, the server also keeps your Apple account identifier with it. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the ID is created or joined the app may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; we receive Apple's attestation and nothing about you. The ID is sent with some requests as a sign-in token, and on the More Apps screen it shows which Midgar apps you already use. It is not removed by Delete Account in Psywave, because your other Midgar apps share it. To have it deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

Purchases

Psywave Pro (an auto-renewing subscription) and credit packs are sold through Apple In-App Purchase. Each purchase carries your anonymous Midgar ID as Apple's app account token. The app sends the signed transaction record Apple issues (which product, the price, the dates and Apple's transaction identifiers) to Midgar's purchase ledger at api.midgarcorp.cc, which keeps it linked to your Midgar ID so that Psywave Pro is recognised on all your devices and your purchases can be restored in your other Midgar apps, and Apple's own server notifications about your purchases reach the same ledger. Our server checks the signed transaction of a credit pack before it credits your balance. From version 1.4.4, neither the app nor our servers send anything about your purchases to RevenueCat; earlier versions have RevenueCat validate purchases, and it receives the purchase receipt from Apple, an anonymous identifier that it generates and your IP address, but not your name or email address. We never receive your payment-card details.

On-device data

Content you create in the app, such as your playlist history and a small copy of any photo you made a playlist from, is stored on your device. It reaches our server only inside the requests described under AI processing and Apple Music, or when you tap Share Link (see Shared playlists), and apart from a shared playlist it is not kept there. It is included in your iCloud or computer backups, as the rest of the app's data is. A diagnostic log is written there too, recording errors and other messages the app logs, including playlist names and song titles and artists, with the app's version and build at launch, but never your descriptions or any token. The log is left out of backups. It leaves your device only if you choose to share it with Share Diagnostics.

Shared playlists

When you tap Share Link, the playlist's name, description, mood and genre, the storefront (the two-letter country code of your device's region), and song list (titles, artists, albums, Apple Music song identifiers, and album-art addresses) are stored on our server so the link can show them. Anyone with the link can view that page. The page never shows your photo or your identity, and apart from the storefront it carries nothing else from your device. To have a shared page removed, contact us with its link.

Apple Music

With your permission, Psywave finds songs in the Apple Music catalog and adds the playlists you make to your Apple Music library. If you turn on Psywave Pro's “Only songs new to me” or “Grow One of My Playlists”, the app reads your library on your device to skip songs you already have or to see which songs a chosen playlist contains; the chosen playlist's name and the song titles and artists of that playlist, together with your Apple Music country, your device's language and your discovery setting, are sent to our AI provider to suggest additions, and nothing from your library is stored on our servers.

More Apps

Settings has a Midgar universe card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. It fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings; your App Store country code is sent so prices are correct for your region. It reports which app tiles were displayed and which were tapped to api.midgarcorp.cc. That report contains no identifier of any kind and not your Midgar ID: only the event, the promoted app's App Store id, your App Store country code, where in the app the screen was opened, and the app's name, version and platform. Our server stores a running total per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor), so there is no per-person or per-device record. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking. The Midgar ID is also sent as a sign-in token with some requests to Midgar's AI and sharing service, such as making or sharing a playlist, and with the request that lists which Midgar apps you already use. When you tap an app you do not have and that runs on this device, its App Store page opens inside Psywave with Midgar's campaign and provider tokens, so Apple can attribute the visit to Midgar.

The app also sends three more kinds of anonymous count to Midgar's servers in the same way: one "active" count the first time you use the app on each day, in each week and in each month, which tells us how many people use the app; one count each time a paywall is shown, labelled only with where it appeared; and one count for each purchase that names only the product bought. Like the More Apps report, they carry no identifier and are stored only as running totals per day, event, app, platform and app version (major and minor), with the placement or product named where there is one, and an active count also carries the ISO week in which the app was installed. They are used only to understand how the app is used, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar's server runs on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs such as your IP address for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. It contains no third-party advertising and no user-to-user communication.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show third-party advertising inside the app, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your data to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your playlist history: on your device and in your device backups, until you delete the app or your account.
  • The diagnostic log: only on your device, until you delete the app or your account.
  • Your anonymous account, its credit balance and request records: until you delete your account in Settings.
  • The Midgar ID record: until you ask us to delete it, except that an identity App Attest never verified, with no Sign in with Apple link and no session still valid, is deleted automatically 30 days after it was created.
  • Shared playlist pages: until you ask us to remove them or delete your account.
  • Purchase records: Midgar's purchase ledger keeps them as financial records, linked to your Midgar ID until that ID is deleted, after which nothing links them to a live ID. RevenueCat keeps the records it received from earlier versions of the app under its own policy. Apple's records of your purchases: kept by Apple.

Your choices and rights

You can delete your account in Settings at any time, which removes your anonymous account and everything saved on the device. Deleting the app alone removes the on-device data but not the server-side account. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information to provide the playlists and purchases you ask for. Processing takes place in the European Union and the United States, by Cloudflare as our hosting provider, by Anthropic as our AI provider and, if our AI settings cannot be read, by Google Gemini as a fallback AI provider. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
