SForesight Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

SForesight was a Mac app that found the SF Symbol you meant from a description. On 30 October 2026 it went off sale, and its symbol search service was retired. It has no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking. What reaches Midgar's servers is an anonymous Midgar ID that also works in Midgar's other apps, anonymous usage counts, and the description you type if you press Search, which our server now refuses. The symbol you copy goes to your Mac's clipboard and nowhere else.

In short

  • The description you type is sent to our server only after you press Search, and if you pressed Search while offline, once the connection returns. Search was retired on 30 October 2026, so our server refuses it and passes it on to no one.
  • Our server does not keep your description or the suggestions. It writes one log line per request with the time, the status and a request number, never the text.
  • The app keeps one anonymous Midgar ID that works across Midgar's apps. It holds no name or email address about you.
  • The app sends anonymous usage counts and portal events to our server. They carry no Midgar ID and no device identifier. The app has no setting to turn them off.
  • SForesight was a paid download from the Mac App Store until 30 October 2026. It has no in-app purchases. The Midgar portal reads from our server which purchases you hold in Midgar's other apps.
  • Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, nothing is sold, and no data is given to third parties for their own use.

Who we are

SForesight is published by Midgar Oy, a company in Finland, which is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

AI processing

Symbol search used an AI model to suggest SF Symbols from a description. It was retired on 30 October 2026, and the app no longer shows any suggestions. The app still has the search screen, and pressing Search still sends the description you type, over an encrypted connection, to our server at api.midgarcorp.cc. Our server refuses that request, because the service no longer exists, and it passes the description on to no one. Our server does not keep your description or the answer. The request carries the app's name and version and a new random request number. It carries no Midgar ID and no token. Until 30 October 2026, our server added our own instructions (suggest ten SF Symbols) and passed the description to OpenAI's API. OpenAI handled it under its own privacy and security commitments and may keep API requests for a limited time under its terms. The description was used only to produce your suggestions and was not used to train AI models. Until the retirement, the server also received the IP address of your connection. Our gateway used that address, through Cloudflare's rate-limit service, to limit how many requests one address could make each minute, and the search service kept its own count of requests per minute in its memory. Midgar did not store the address. Until 30 October 2026, the app checked every suggested name against the list of symbols that ships with it and showed only names that exist.

Identity

SForesight works without an account or sign-in. On launch the app asks our server for an anonymous Midgar ID: a random identifier, and a random secret that proves the ID is yours. The server keeps the ID, a hash of the secret, the date it was created and the app that created it. For each app install that attested with App Attest, the server also keeps the public key of that install's attestation key, Apple's attestation receipt when it sends one, a signature counter, the environment and the date the key was last used. It also keeps a hash of each refresh token, with its app and dates. The app keeps both in your iCloud Keychain, so the same ID follows you across your own devices and across Midgar's other apps. When the ID is created or joined the app may ask Apple's App Attest service to confirm that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app; we receive Apple's attestation and nothing about you. The server holds no name or email address about you. A Midgar ID linked through Sign in with Apple also keeps Apple's account identifier for that link. Searching does not use the ID. The ID is sent as a sign-in token with the two requests the Midgar portal makes when you open it from Settings: one that lists the Midgar apps that hold a session for your ID, and one that lists the purchases and subscriptions your ID holds in Midgar's other apps, so the portal can show them. When the app joins a Midgar ID that another Midgar app on this Mac already holds, or when its session has to be started again, the ID and its secret are sent to our server so the app can sign in. To have the ID deleted, email marcus@midgarcorp.cc.

Usage counts

When SForesight starts and each time it comes to the front, it sends an anonymous count to api.midgarcorp.cc that the app was active. It sends one count for the day, one for the ISO week and one for the month, and only the first time in each of those periods. Each count carries the week this copy of the app was installed on this Mac, the platform (macOS) and the app version, of which our server keeps only the major and minor number. The counts carry no Midgar ID, no device identifier and no token, and our server stores them without IP addresses. Our server adds them to daily totals per app, event, platform, app version (major and minor number only), install week and period (day, week or month). The app has no setting to turn these counts off.

Purchase

SForesight was bought once on the Mac App Store, and Apple handles the payment. The app contains no in-app purchases, no subscription and no purchase code of its own, and it sends us nothing about your purchase. When you open the Midgar portal from Settings, the app asks our server, with your Midgar ID, which purchases and subscriptions you hold in Midgar's other apps, so the portal can show them on your Mac. Midgar's server keeps the purchase records of Midgar's other apps that are linked to your Midgar ID, including the product, purchase date, price, currency and storefront.

On-device data

On your Mac, the app keeps the number of symbols you have copied in its preferences. Its Midgar folder in Application Support keeps the day you first ran the app, how many times it has started, when the app last asked you to rate it, so it never asks too often, the usage counts not yet sent, which counts were already sent for each day, week and month, and the week this copy was installed. A diagnostic log is written on your Mac too, recording errors, counts and the app version, never your descriptions or any token. It does not leave your Mac unless you send it to us yourself.

More Apps

The Settings window has a Midgar universe card that opens a view of Midgar's other apps. Once a day, when Settings shows the card, the app fetches the app list from midgar-catalog.guitaripod.workers.dev and asks Apple's public iTunes lookup service for current icons, prices and ratings. Opening that view fetches them again. Those requests include the region set in your Mac's language and region settings, so prices are correct for your region. Opening the view sends an anonymous event to api.midgarcorp.cc, and so does each app tile that is shown, tapped, loaded or opened from it. Each app event (impression, tap, loaded, launch or overlay) carries its name, the promoted app's App Store id, that region, where in the app it happened, and the app's name, version and platform. The event for opening the view carries its name, that region, where in the app it happened, and the app's name, version and platform, but no app id. These events carry no Midgar ID and no device identifier. Our server keeps running totals per day, event, app and platform, with the app version (major and minor number only), and for each event the place in the app and, for app events, the promoted app, so there is no per-person or per-device record. Our server does not store the region. This is product-interaction data, used only to decide which of our apps to promote, never for advertising or tracking.

Midgar's server runs on Cloudflare, which may keep standard request logs such as your IP address for a limited time. We do not use them for anything else.

Midgar may publish aggregate statistics built from anonymous counts, such as weekly active installs, which never identify a person.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. It contains no advertising and no user-to-user communication.

What we don't do

We do not sell your data, show advertising inside the app, use advertising identifiers, run third-party analytics or crash-reporting software, or ask for tracking permission. We do not use your descriptions to train AI models.

How long we keep data

  • Your descriptions: not kept by our server. Before 30 October 2026, they also went to OpenAI, which handled them under its own terms and may keep API requests for a limited time.
  • Your counters, the usage counts not yet sent and the log: only on your Mac, until you delete the app.
  • Usage counts that were sent: kept by our server as daily totals, with no identifier in them.
  • The Midgar ID record and its keys: until you ask us to delete it. An ID that was never verified by Apple's App Attest, has no Apple sign-in link, was created more than 30 days ago and has no session that is still valid is deleted automatically. Expired refresh tokens are removed when the ID next refreshes its session.
  • Purchase records of Midgar's other apps that are linked to your Midgar ID: kept by our server with no set period. Deleting the Midgar ID does not remove them; they stay as Apple's financial records that no live Midgar ID can reach.

Your choices and rights

Nothing you type leaves your Mac until you press Search. Where privacy law such as the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or move information, or to object to its use, contact us at marcus@midgarcorp.cc and we will help. We process this information only to operate the services described above. Processing takes place by Cloudflare as our hosting provider, in data centres that may be outside the European Union and the United States, and, for descriptions sent before 30 October 2026, by OpenAI as our AI provider. You may complain to your local data protection authority; in Finland that is the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi).
